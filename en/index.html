Memorising is only the beginning. Keeping it is the real challenge.

Itqān takes care of the planning. The practice is up to you.

A day with Itqān

Practise in a few simple steps every day

١

Start

Today shows what is due: your wird, meaning the reviews due today, and what you are memorising new. You set your daily amount yourself, per weekday if you like.

٢

Recite

You see the page as in the Muṣḥaf, with the text covered, and recite from memory. If you get stuck, “First word” helps, or you look over the section first.

٣

Check

Then you uncover the text and rate yourself verse by verse: Correct, Hesitated or Mistake. The more honestly you rate, the better your reviews fit.

٤

Finish

Itqān points, your streak of completed days and your ḥasanāt, counted by the letters you recite, show that you keep at it. You can hide the ḥasanāt if you like.

A method that adapts

Itqān follows what teachers of ḥifẓ, memorising the Qurʾān, have passed on for generations: the Qurʾān stays when you review it at intervals, and more often where it is still shaky. Learning research confirms it.

Five steps for every section

Every section grows through five steps: new, building, consolidated, secure and finally mutqan, mastered with precision. Flawless rounds raise the step, mistakes lower it.

Intervals that grow

A section comes back at growing intervals, for example after 1, 3 and 7 days. What sits firmly you see much later: Itqān estimates for every section when it is due again, so firm material comes less often and shaky material more often. A mistake, hesitation or word help brings a section back sooner.

Mistakes are clues

You tap the word and choose the kind of mistake. This builds your list of trouble spots for focused practice. If you strayed into a similar passage, Itqān shows you that group of similar verses first.

The Muṣḥaf as you know it

True to every page, in the script of the Madinah Muṣḥaf: 604 pages, 30 juzʾ, 114 sūrahs. You practise on exactly the page you know from reading.

Colour that helps you remember

Itqān colours what your eye should remember. Drag the slider to compare the same page without and with colour.

  • Sections

    Every section has its own tint and fine boundary lines, so you see at a glance where a passage begins and ends.

  • Verse markers

    In gold, with a clear number. Your eye remembers where a verse ends, and with it the whole page.

  • Pause signs

    In teal, clearly set apart from the text.

If you prefer it calmer, turn the colours off or choose a softer variant.

For learners everywhere

Itqān comes in seven languages, with Arabic and Urdu written right to left. The whole app adapts: texts, dates, numbers and the names of the sūrahs. Tap an āya and its translation appears if you like, in six languages.

Your data is yours

Itqān works without an account and with no ads, and offline once it has loaded. If you practise on several devices, you can create an optional account: you sign in with a code sent by email, no password needed, and your data is kept on a server in Frankfurt. You can export or delete it at any time.
