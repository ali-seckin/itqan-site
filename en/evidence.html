Evidence and limits

What the learning model supports and what it does not

Designed on evidence, not proven. Itqān builds on well-studied principles of learning. Whether this plan improves how well you retain the Qurʾān we have not yet tested in a study of our own with people.

What the learning model does

  • Intervals grow with your skill. What sits firmly comes back less often, what wobbles comes back more often.
  • A mistake leads to a check the next day. This applies when about a quarter of the verses in a section, or more, were wrong.
  • The round stays. About a quarter of the day’s places go to the section that has waited longest, so nothing is left uncared for.
  • The first check comes first. Sections you learned earlier that Itqān has never checked get many places until each has had its turn.
  • The Hifz pace protects revision. A new lesson only begins when no more than your daily amount of revision is waiting today and at most three lessons are running.

What it rests on

Research on spaced repetition and on the forgetting curve. It shows that recalling from memory and then comparing with the text helps you retain more, and that reviews at suitable intervals hold better than crammed ones. These studies mostly involve school and university students learning vocabulary, facts and prose, not the Qurʾān.

We also tested the plan in simulations: in 66 independently built test worlds in which memory forgets in different ways. The plan does not know their rules. It was compared with the earlier modes Classic and Long term.

Result: depending on the daily amount (2, 3, 5 or 8 sections), the plan retained 1.3 to 3.7 percentage points more than Classic and 0.2 to 5.6 percentage points more than Long term in the second simulated year. That is a moderate lead, largest when time is short. At eight sections a day the plan is practically level with Long term.

The plan has weaknesses too: in individual world types it fell up to 3.1 percentage points behind the best earlier mode, for example with a different shape of forgetting or when longer intervals bring the memory nothing. The weakest ten percent of sections did slightly better under Long term, because the plan keeps places free for older sections.

All figures come from simulations. What was measured is how likely it is that a section is recited without a mistake. The figures describe constructed worlds, not you.

What is not proven

  • There is no proof in real people. We know of no study that compares revision plans for memorising the Qurʾān.
  • The simulation results hold for the modelled worlds. Only real data will show whether reality lies within them.
  • Many numbers in the model are reasoned assumptions, for example how much firmness a section gains after a success. They come mostly from research with flashcards, not from the Qurʾān.
  • The model learns from your own rating. Anyone who hides mistakes harms themselves most: the weakest spots then stay hidden for longer.
  • Still open are similar passages (Mutashabihat), the effect during Ramadan and during illness, and learning in later life.

How it is checked

  • Voluntary learning log. With your consent the app sends one line per exercise under a random number, without name, email address or recording. You find it in Settings under Research and can withdraw it at any time.
  • Reassessment after one year. Then we check the model against this data and change it where it does not hold up.
  • Randomised comparison (planned). Two plans are assigned at random to sections of the same person. After eight to twelve weeks the sections are recited without warning and rated by a teacher who does not know the assignment.

What Itqān does not promise

  • Itqān does not replace a teacher. Someone who teaches the Qurʾān and listens to your recitation stays irreplaceable.
  • Itqān does not guarantee any particular success in memorising. The plan helps you revise, but the revising itself stays your task.
  • The percentages on this page describe simulations. They are no promise for your own learning.
